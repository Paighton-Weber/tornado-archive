The Science Behind Tornadoes

Learn how tornadoes form, how their strength is measured, and how modern radar helps meteorologists detect dangerous storms.

How Tornadoes Form

Tornadoes usually form from powerful thunderstorms called supercells. These storms can develop rotating columns of air when warm, moist air near the ground meets cooler, drier air above it. When wind speed and direction change with height, the storm may begin rotating.

Learn more about tornado formation and forecasting from NOAA .

What Makes a Tornado Dangerous?

A tornado becomes especially dangerous when strong rotation reaches the ground and produces violent winds. These winds can destroy buildings, flip vehicles, and turn debris into dangerous projectiles.

The Enhanced Fujita Scale

The Enhanced Fujita Scale, also called the EF Scale, measures tornado intensity based on damage. Tornadoes are rated from EF0, which causes light damage, to EF5, which causes incredible destruction.

EF0Light
EF1Moderate
EF2Considerable
EF3Severe
EF4Devastating
EF5Incredible

Contributions to Modern Tornado Science

Historic tornadoes have helped scientists better understand how tornadoes form, how they move, and how communities can prepare for them. Damage surveys after major tornadoes allow meteorologists to study wind patterns, storm paths, and the types of structures most vulnerable to destruction.

Events such as the Tri-State Tornado, the 1974 Super Outbreak, the 1999 Moore tornado, and the 2011 Super Outbreak contributed to improvements in storm warnings, radar technology, emergency communication, and public safety planning. Modern Doppler radar now allows meteorologists to detect rotation inside storms, giving people more time to seek shelter before a tornado reaches the ground.

Learn more about current tornado research from the National Severe Storms Laboratory.

Radar and Warning Improvements

Before modern radar, many tornado warnings depended heavily on eyewitness reports. Today, Doppler radar helps meteorologists identify rotating storms, track storm movement, and issue warnings more quickly. This has made tornado forecasting more accurate and has helped reduce the number of people caught off guard by severe weather.
